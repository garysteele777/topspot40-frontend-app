About TopSpot40

Why I created TopSpot40, and why music discovery still matters.

Growing Up with Radio

TopSpot40 began with memories of growing up on an Indiana farm, where radio was more than background noise. Programs like Casey Kasem's American Top 40 did more than play songs. They created anticipation, told stories, introduced artists, and made music feel like a journey.

The Story Behind the Name

People occasionally ask where the name TopSpot40 came from. The answer reflects the three major influences behind the project.

The word Top refers to the ranked music programs that form the foundation of TopSpot40. Like the countdown shows I enjoyed growing up, songs are organized into ranked lists that highlight memorable tracks from each decade, genre, or collection.

The word Spot is a nod to Spotify. TopSpot40 can open selected tracks in Spotify, while Spotify handles music playback, advertisements, availability, account restrictions, and Free or Premium behavior. TopSpot40 adds storytelling, artist biographies, music history, and discovery.

The number 40 comes from the influence of Casey Kasem's American Top 40 countdowns. Those programs inspired my love of music rankings, artist stories, and the excitement of discovering what song would come next.

Put together, the name TopSpot40 represents ranked music discovery, direct Spotify links for listening, and the enduring influence of the Top 40 radio tradition that inspired the project.

I wanted to bring some of that feeling back. Streaming services make it easy to hear almost any song instantly, but the stories behind the songs are often missing. TopSpot40 was created to add the context, history, artist information, and storytelling that make music more meaningful.

Music, Memory, and Older Generations

One of my hopes for TopSpot40 is that it can help older generations reconnect with the songs that shaped their lives. A favorite song can bring back memories of childhood, first dates, military service, weddings, family gatherings, church, road trips, and moments that might otherwise have faded.

Music has a special ability to connect people across generations. TopSpot40 is my way of honoring those memories while helping younger listeners discover the music, artists, and stories that came before them.

Patty's Rule

My wife Patty has played an important role in shaping TopSpot40 from the very beginning. Fluent in English, Spanish, and Portuguese, she brought a unique perspective to the project as both a music lover and a multilingual listener.

Over time, I developed what I jokingly call "Patty's Rule": if Patty cannot easily understand or enjoy a feature, it probably needs to be simplified. That simple principle helped keep TopSpot40 focused on the listener rather than the technology. As an engineer, I naturally enjoy building systems and adding features, but Patty consistently reminded me that the experience should remain intuitive, enjoyable, and accessible.

Patty also played a vital role in one of the most distinctive parts of TopSpot40—its narrated storytelling. As we explored the growing library of ElevenLabs voices, she helped evaluate and select many of the voices used throughout the platform. Her fluency in all three supported languages allowed her to judge pronunciation, pacing, tone, and overall authenticity in ways that I could not have done alone.

The result is that TopSpot40 does not simply tell stories in English, Spanish, and Portuguese—it tells them in voices carefully chosen to sound natural and engaging to listeners in each language. Many of the voices heard throughout the platform were selected with Patty's guidance and encouragement.

In many ways, Patty helped shape not only how TopSpot40 works, but also how it sounds.

Patty's Rule: If a feature is too complicated to enjoy, it probably needs to be simplified.

Teaching, Isaiah, and the Python Class

TopSpot40 also grew out of my work with the Pharr Fetched Coders group at the Pharr Memorial Library. What started as a local Python programming class became connected to a real software project.

Through that group, I met Isaiah, a computer science student at the University of Texas Rio Grande Valley. Working with him reminded me that learning is a two-way street. I have been able to share decades of experience in programming, engineering, and project development, while Isaiah has brought fresh ideas, modern web development skills, and a new perspective to the platform.

Music Without Borders

My life and career have also shaped the multilingual side of TopSpot40. I am bilingual in English and Spanish, and somewhat fluent in Portuguese after working in Angola, Africa as a Six Sigma consultant. Those experiences expanded my appreciation for the ways music connects people across languages and cultures.

That is one reason TopSpot40 supports English, Spanish, and Portuguese. Music may be a universal language, but I wanted the stories behind the music to be accessible too.

A Personal Journey

What began as a software project gradually became something much more personal. Along the way, TopSpot40 introduced me to artists, stories, cultures, and musical traditions that I might never have explored otherwise.

In that sense, TopSpot40 has been almost a spiritual journey for me. It has deepened my appreciation for music, history, heritage, creativity, and the experiences that connect us across generations.

Discovery Never Ends

One of my favorite discoveries came from my own project. While exploring music and artists for TopSpot40, I came across B.B. King and realized I knew very little about him, despite his enormous influence on blues and popular music.

That discovery led me to learn about his remarkable career, his influence on generations of musicians, and his connection to artists such as Buddy Guy. In fact, learning through TopSpot40 inspired me to plan a trip to see Buddy Guy perform in person.

If TopSpot40 has taught me anything, it is that discovery never ends. There is always another artist to learn about, another story to hear, another culture to appreciate, and another song waiting to become a favorite.

Why TopSpot40 Is Different

TopSpot40 is not a streaming service. It is an interactive music discovery guide and companion that can open selected tracks in Spotify.

Most music platforms focus on helping listeners find and play songs. TopSpot40 focuses on helping listeners connect with the stories, artists, history, memories, and emotions behind the music.

Streaming technology has made music more available than ever before. A listener can search for almost any song and hear it within seconds. But availability is not the same as connection. Sometimes the missing piece is context: who created the song, why it mattered, what was happening at the time, and how it fits into the larger story of music history.

Spotify handles music playback, advertisements, availability, account restrictions, and Free or Premium behavior. TopSpot40 helps listeners connect more deeply with the music.

Storytelling Around the Music

TopSpot40 surrounds the listening experience with narrated introductions, artist biographies, historical background, rankings, cultural context, and curated discovery programs. The goal is to make listening feel more like a guided journey than a simple playlist.

Discovery With Meaning

TopSpot40 is designed for both nostalgia and discovery. A listener may begin with a favorite song from the past and end up learning about an artist, a musical tradition, a cultural movement, or a forgotten piece of history.

Multilingual Music Stories

Artist stories and discovery content are available in English, Spanish, and Portuguese. This allows listeners to experience the same music history and storytelling across languages and cultures.

Connection, Not Consumption

TopSpot40 is not designed to encourage people to consume more music as quickly as possible. It is designed to slow the experience down enough to notice the stories, memories, and meaning behind the songs.

TopSpot40 is not designed to help people listen to more music. It is designed to help people connect more deeply with the music they already love.

Four Ways to Listen

TopSpot40 offers four distinct listening experiences, each designed to help listeners discover music in a different way.

1. Nostalgia Programs

Nostalgia Programs recreate the experience of classic countdown radio by organizing music by decade and genre. Each program contains ranked songs, narrated introductions, artist information, historical context, and a button that opens the selected track in Spotify.

Examples include:

  • 1950s Country
  • 1960s Rock
  • 1970s Pop
  • 1980s Country
  • 1990s Latin Global
  • 2000s R&B Soul

2. Collections

Collections organize music around themes, traditions, cultures, historical topics, and shared experiences. Unlike Nostalgia Programs, Collections are not limited to a single decade or genre. Instead, they bring together songs connected by a common story.

Examples include:

  • Railroad & Train Songs
  • Traditional Hymns
  • Patriotic Favorites
  • Great American Songbook
  • Mexican-American Favorites
  • Cowboy Songs & Western Favorites

3. Artist Spotlight

Artist Spotlight focuses on individual artists. Featured Artists include narrated biographies, historical background, career highlights, and curated track selections designed to tell the story of an artist's life and music.

TopSpot40 includes more than 329 Featured Artists with narrated biographies available in English, Spanish, and Portuguese.

Examples include:

  • B.B. King
  • Johnny Cash
  • Selena
  • Willie Nelson
  • Loretta Lynn
  • Frank Sinatra

4. Radio Modes

Radio Modes combine narration, artist information, storytelling, rankings, and selected-track Spotify buttons into a guided listening experience. TopSpot40 plays its own narration first, then the listener chooses whether to open the selected track in Spotify.

A Vision for TopSpot40

Looking beyond technology toward connection, discovery, learning, and preserving musical heritage.

TopSpot40 began as a personal software project, but over time it grew into something much larger. What started as an effort to combine music, storytelling, and technology gradually became a vision for helping people reconnect with the music and memories that have shaped their lives.

Music and Memory

One of the audiences I hope to serve is older adults, including retirees, Winter Texans, and assisted living communities. Many people in these groups grew up during some of the most influential decades in popular music history. The songs they remember are often connected to important moments in their lives, and hearing those songs can unlock memories that may have been dormant for years.

A favorite song can bring back memories of childhood, military service, first dates, weddings, church gatherings, family traditions, road trips, and lifelong friendships. Music has a remarkable ability to reconnect us with experiences that helped shape who we are.

Conversations and Connection

Music often creates opportunities for conversation. A single song can lead to stories about where someone grew up, who influenced them, what they experienced, and how the world has changed over time.

My hope is that TopSpot40 can become more than a music discovery platform. I hope it becomes a catalyst for conversation, learning, memory sharing, and human connection.

Lifelong Discovery

TopSpot40 is not only about revisiting the past. It is also about continuing to learn. Younger listeners can discover artists and traditions from earlier generations, while older listeners can continue exploring music, cultures, and stories they may never have encountered before.

One of the great surprises of building TopSpot40 has been realizing how much there is still to learn. Every artist biography leads to another artist. Every collection leads to another story. Every genre opens the door to another chapter of music history.

Discovery Never Ends.

Music Without Borders

TopSpot40 currently supports storytelling in English, Spanish, and Portuguese. This reflects my own experiences living and working across cultures and my belief that music has the power to connect people across languages, generations, and backgrounds.

By making artist stories and music history available in multiple languages, TopSpot40 seeks to make discovery accessible to a wider audience while celebrating the rich diversity of musical traditions around the world.

Looking Ahead

The future of TopSpot40 is not defined by technology alone. It is defined by curiosity, connection, storytelling, and a desire to help people experience music in a deeper and more meaningful way.

Whether someone is hearing a favorite song from childhood, learning the story behind an artist for the first time, or discovering an entirely new genre of music, there is always another connection waiting to be made.

That is the future I imagine for TopSpot40.

B.B. King Artist Story

Complete featured artist biography example • 8 minute narrated story
This complete artist story is included as an example of the narrated biography content available in TopSpot40. Artist stories are available in English, Spanish, and Portuguese.

The Story of Bb King

B.B. King never just played the blues—he seemed to carry on a conversation with them, one note at a time. You’d watch him on stage, swaying gently side to side, eyes half-closed, and it was clear the guitar wasn’t just an instrument. It was a partner. And nothing tells you more about who he was than the night that partner got her name.

It was the winter of 1949 in a little place called Twist, Arkansas. The dance hall was packed, the music was loud, and two men got into it over a woman. In the scuffle they knocked over a barrel of kerosene that was being used to heat the room. Flames raced across the floor. Everyone stampeded for the exits, including B.B. But halfway across the parking lot he realized he’d left his guitar inside. Without hesitating he ran back into the burning building, dodging flames, coughing through smoke, and came out with the instrument tucked under his arm like a rescued child. The next morning he learned the fight had been about a woman named Lucille. From that day forward every guitar he ever owned would bear her name—a private joke, a cautionary tale, and a love story all at once. “Lucille taught me a lesson,” he’d say with that rich chuckle of his. “Don’t run into burning buildings, and don’t fight over women.”

That mix of warmth, humor, and hard-headed honesty ran through everything he touched. He was born Riley King on a Mississippi cotton plantation in 1925, lost his mother when he was nine, and was essentially raised by his grandmother. The church gave him his first taste of singing, but the blues found him in the fields. He’d chop cotton all day under that merciless Delta sun, then sit on the porch at night trying to make an old pawn-shop guitar sound like the records he heard on the radio. T-Bone Walker’s electric guitar hit him like a revelation—the way one note could cut right through you. He practiced until his fingers bled, copying every lick he could catch.

By the late forties he had made his way to Memphis, sleeping on a friend’s floor and working days at a cotton gin while haunting Beale Street at night. The WDIA radio station gave him his first real break. They let the young man with the big voice spin records and soon let him perform live on air. The program director started calling him the Beale Street Blues Boy. The name got shortened to B.B., and it stuck. From that little radio studio his sound began to travel across the South, reaching people who recognized their own lives in the stories he sang.

What always amazed me was how much road he put behind him. For decades he played three hundred-plus dates a year. Not festival sets with light shows—real one-nighters in juke joints, VFW halls, and small theaters where the dressing room might be a broom closet. His big custom bus, which he affectionately called home, rolled through every kind of weather and every kind of town. He used to say the audience deserved the best he had whether there were fifty people or five thousand. That work ethic came from the cotton fields and never left him.

One night in 1964 at Chicago’s Regal Theater, that dedication paid off in a moment he would talk about for the rest of his life. The crowd was already on fire before he played a single chord. When he hit the opening notes of “Every Day I Have the Blues,” the place exploded. You can still hear it on the recording—women screaming, men shouting encouragement like they were at a Baptist revival. B.B. stood there letting Lucille cry, then answered her with his own voice, and the conversation between artist and audience became something almost sacred. That live album became a cornerstone for a generation of young musicians who were about to change rock and roll.

A few years later came another turning point that still gives me chills. The Fillmore West in San Francisco, 1967. Bill Graham had booked B.B. for what was then a mostly white, mostly young rock audience. B.B. was nervous. These kids didn’t grow up on his music. What if they didn’t get it? He walked out, looked at the sea of long-haired hippies, and then something beautiful happened. They rose to their feet before he even struck a note. When he finally played, the response was so overwhelming he had to stop between songs to wipe his eyes. In that moment the bridge was crossed. The boy from the Mississippi Delta had brought the blues to the children of the Summer of Love, and they recognized truth when they heard it. Eric Clapton, Stevie Ray Vaughan, and a hundred others would later say the same thing in different ways: we all traced our sound back to B.B.

He carried himself with a dignity that never felt forced. After shows he would stand in the lobby signing autographs until the last person left. He remembered names. He asked about your family. There’s a famous story from the road where a young promoter tried to slip him an extra hundred dollars under the table. B.B. politely refused it, saying, “Son, if you can’t pay me what we agreed on, just tell me. But don’t ever try to sneak money to me like I’m some kind of hustler.” That same man would turn around and quietly pay medical bills for members of his band or help a struggling blues musician get new tires for his car. The King took care of his own.

Even in his later years, when diabetes tried to slow him down, he kept moving. He’d sit on his bus between shows eating pork rinds—his guilty pleasure—and trading stories with the band. He loved to laugh, loved good barbecue, and loved introducing young players to the audience. When he stepped on stage with U2 in the late eighties and traded verses on “When Love Comes to Town,” you could see the pure joy in his face. The kid from the cotton patch was still discovering new friends through music.

And that’s the magic that still works today. When the world feels too loud, too fast, too cold, you can put on B.B. King and everything gets simpler. One guitar, one voice, and a lifetime of feeling poured into every phrase. He showed us that the blues isn’t about feeling sorry for yourself. It’s about taking the hard parts of life—lost love, long roads, smoky rooms, burning buildings—and turning them into something that makes other people feel less alone.

That’s why Lucille is still singing. That’s why we still need the King. Because somewhere out there tonight, someone is hearing “The Thrill Is Gone” for the first time and realizing their pain has company. And somewhere else, an old soul is smiling because those familiar notes feel like coming home. B.B. King didn’t just play the blues. He befriended them, wrestled with them, and in the end made them beautiful for all of us. And every time Lucille bends a note just so, you can almost hear him say, with that warm voice and that knowing laugh, “We’re gonna be all right.”

What began as a software project eventually became something much more personal. Along the way, I discovered artists, cultures, musical traditions, and stories that I might never have encountered otherwise. Every collection seemed to open another door. Every artist biography led to another artist. Every song revealed another piece of history waiting to be explored.

TopSpot40 has taught me that curiosity does not have an age limit. Whether we are eighteen or eighty, there is always something new to discover.

My hope is that this project encourages listeners not only to enjoy the music they already know, but also to remain curious about the artists, stories, cultures, and history behind it.

Discovery Never Ends.

Thank you for joining me on this journey.
